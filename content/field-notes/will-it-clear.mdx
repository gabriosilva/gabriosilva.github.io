## Something to try

Start the ball at the top and drag the block further away, a little at a time. At some point the ball can no longer clear it, even though from the top it lands well beyond where the block is.<Note n={1}>In this model, from the top of the ramp the ball lands around x = 570, but no block placed beyond about x = 476 can be cleared.</Note>

## The rules of this sketch

- The ball rolls without slipping, so some of its energy goes into spinning; it leaves the lip at about 85% of the speed a sliding ball would.
- It leaves the lip at 38° upward and then follows a simple arc, with no air resistance.
- If it touches the block, it bounces back and settles. If it lands before the block and rolls into it, that doesn’t count: the block has to be cleared in the air.

## Open questions

- What would a steeper lip change: further flights, or higher ones?
- Is there a block position where almost every starting height works, and one where only a single height does?
